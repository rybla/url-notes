  • Model: Analyzes a continuous-time, long-term employment relationship between a risk-neutral principal and a risk-neutral, overconfident worker.
  • Beliefs & Learning: The worker is overconfident about his own talent (match quality) but updates his beliefs using Bayes' rule. Talent is binary (high/low). The absence of a positive performance signal causes pessimism to grow.
  • Exploitation Dynamic: The firm exploits the worker's overconfidence. This exploitation can intensify over time as negative signals accumulate. Once the worker's high talent is proven, exploitation opportunities cease.
  • The Peter Principle: The model provides a microfoundation for the Peter Principle. It can be optimal to promote a successful worker to an unrelated task because success in the current role resolves uncertainty and ends exploitation. A new role re-introduces uncertainty and belief divergence, creating fresh opportunities to exploit overconfidence.
  • Contribution: This explanation does not rely on unverifiable performance, aligning with empirical findings where the Peter Principle is observed in roles with easily measured output (e.g., sales).
